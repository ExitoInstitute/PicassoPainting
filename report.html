COMPREHENSIVE CONTENT PROPOSAL FOR PICASSO PAINTING

Strategy, rationale, and preliminary five-week plan

01What I want to achieve

My proposal is to use content to attract homeowners, help them make better decisions, and turn that interest into estimate requests for Picasso Painting.

I want to pursue three connected objectives:

  1. Discovery: help people who do not yet know Picasso find relevant content.
  2. Trust: help them understand the expertise and care behind the work.
  3. Conversion: make it clear how to get in touch and request an estimate.

To achieve this, I propose combining 15 Reels, five carousels, and supporting Stories over five weeks.

This is a complete preliminary plan. The dates and project examples will be adjusted to the available projects and footage.

02Why I chose this strategy

People may become interested in painting before they are ready to hire someone. Sometimes they start with a simple concern: a marked wall, a white color that does not quite feel right, or an entryway that looks worn.

I want to be part of that conversation and help them understand their options.

The structure will be:

Recognizable situation → compelling question → demonstrable explanation → useful action.

For example, explaining why a touch-up in the same color is still visible provides a useful answer while demonstrating professional judgment.

I also want the content to help people understand the value of the service: what is prepared, what is protected, and what is inspected before a job is considered complete.

Picasso’s services page describes preparation, protection of spaces, cleanup, and a final walkthrough. I want to make those steps visible through real examples.

In recent public conversations, I found questions about white paint, how colors appear, estimates, and recommendations for trustworthy painters. These are useful qualitative signals for choosing topics; they are not a measure of overall local demand.

I also reviewed the communications of Palladino Painting, CertaPro San Mateo, and MB Jessee. Preparation, guidance, experience, and project management already appear in their messaging. My proposal for Picasso is to demonstrate its strengths through details and decisions drawn from its own projects.

04What criteria I am applying to the content

I am using the content strategy documents to evaluate four aspects:

  • Hooks: a clear topic, immediate interest, and consistency among on-screen text, spoken words, and visuals.
  • Novelty: a less-obvious angle on a familiar situation.
  • Usefulness: an answer that helps homeowners decide.
  • Credibility: real evidence and clear limits on what is being shown.

Editorial assessments of the ideas help set priorities. I will not present them as guarantees of views or sales.

05Who I want to reach

The proposed primary audience is homeowners considering renovating, maintaining, or improving their homes within Picasso’s service area.

Within that audience, I will focus on three situations:

Situation What they need to understand
They want to improve appearance What could change and what could stay
They have a visible problem What should be checked before painting

Commercial and property-management needs can be added through specific case studies, depending on the business’s priorities.

The public-facing text presented below is proposed in English for the local market. In this version, the strategic explanation is also in English for review.

08The five preliminary carousels

I propose one six-slide carousel per week. Each one will expand on a decision related to the Reels.

C1. Before choosing white paint

Objective: help people compare options and encourage saves.

Slide Proposed text
1 “Choosing white paint? Start with your room.”
2 “Bring a large sample into the space.”
3 “Compare it beside your flooring, trim, and furniture.”
4 “Check it in different areas and at different times of day.”
5 “Compare your final options in the same conditions.”
6 “Save this before choosing your white. Contact Picasso Painting to discuss your project.”

Caption:

“A white paint color should work with your actual space. Compare samples alongside the materials and lighting already in your home. Save this checklist for your next painting project.”

Materials: labeled sample, photos of the space, and consistent shots.

The recommendation to observe samples in different places and lighting conditions is supported by Benjamin Moore’s guidance.

C2. Color and finish: two different decisions

Objective: expand on Reel 09 and help people ask better questions.

Slide Proposed text
1 “Picked the color? There’s another choice to make.”
2 “Your paint selection also includes the finish.”
3 “Compare clearly labeled samples in the same light.”
4 “Look at how each one reflects light across the surface.”
5 “Discuss the appearance, intended use, and product requirements.”
6 “Save this before buying paint. Ask us about the options for your project.”

Caption:

“Keep color and finish on your painting checklist. Review actual samples and the requirements of the product you’re considering before making your final choice.”

Materials: comparable sample panels and product labels. I will confirm the technical details with the team.

C3. What to ask before repainting a surface with problems

Objective: show the importance of assessing the surface before making a recommendation.

Slide Proposed text
1 “The problem came back. What should you ask?”
2 “What condition has actually been identified?”
3 “Does anything need attention before painting?”
4 “What preparation is included in the proposal?”
5 “What result can reasonably be expected?”
6 “Save these questions. Contact Picasso Painting to discuss your painting project.”

Caption:

“A recurring stain, crack, or peeling area deserves a closer look. Start with questions about the condition and the proposed work before deciding on another coat.”

Materials: documented examples. I will not identify causes based on appearance alone.

C4. Questions for the final walkthrough

Objective: help the customer participate in the final walkthrough.

Slide Proposed text
1 “Your painting project is finished. What should you review?”
2 “Walk through the areas included in the agreed scope.”
3 “Point out anything you want to inspect together.”
4 “Ask which product and color information to keep.”
5 “Review the applicable care instructions and remaining questions.”
6 “Save this for your final walkthrough.”

Caption:

“The final walkthrough is a chance to review the completed project and ask questions. Keep this checklist for your next painting job.”

Materials: an actual walkthrough, product information, and documentation without private data.

C5. How to compare painting estimates

Objective: support informed comparisons and generate inquiries.

Slide Proposed text
1 “Comparing painting estimates? Compare the scope.”
2 “Are the same surfaces included?”
3 “What preparation and repairs are specified?”
4 “Which products and coating requirements are included?”
5 “What does each proposal say about coordination, cleanup, and completion?”
6 “Clarify the details before comparing totals. Request a free estimate from Picasso Painting.”

Caption:

“Two painting proposals can include different work. Put the details side by side and ask about anything that needs clarification. Save this before reviewing your estimates.”

Materials: a clearly labeled illustrative estimate or authorized documentation.

09Stories: ten preliminary sequences

I propose posting Stories five days a week. The following sequences provide a starting point for production, avoiding the need to improvise every day.

Each sequence contains three frames. The text will be adapted to the actual visuals available.

S1. Color preference

  1. Show two samples: “Which direction would you explore?”
  2. Poll: “A / B”.
  3. Show both in context: “Compare your favorites in your own space before deciding.”

S2. Following a sample

  1. “Same sample. Morning light.”
  2. “Same sample. Evening light.”
  3. “What do you notice?” with a response box.

S3. Homeowner question

  1. “What’s your biggest painting question?”
  2. Question box: “Ask us here.”
  3. Answer a real question with a short explanation from the team.

If there are no questions yet, I will use a frequently asked question and label it as such.

S4. What to keep and what to change

  1. Show the space: “What would you keep?”
  2. Identify options: “Floor / Furniture / Layout”.
  3. “What would you update first?” with a poll about painted surfaces.

S5. Behind the result

  1. “Here’s where this project started.”
  2. “Here’s one preparation step.”
  3. “Here’s the finished area.”

The sequence will feature the same project throughout.

S6. Clarifying a decision

  1. “Clean, touch up, or repaint?”
  2. Show the area: “This example needs a closer look.”
  3. Explain the actual recommendation and why it was made, without presenting it as a universal solution.

S7. Meet the team

  1. Introduction: name and role.
  2. Question: “What do you check before starting?”
  3. Video answer with an example from the work.

S8. Comparing proposals

  1. “What’s included in your painting estimate?”
  2. Highlight an item: “Preparation / Products / Surfaces”.
  3. “Have a question about your project? Contact us.”

S9. From before to after

  1. Initial photo: “Before”.
  2. Same framing: “After”.
  3. “Which detail would you like to see closer?”

If changes were made beyond painting, I will identify them.

S10. Requesting an estimate

  1. “Thinking about a painting project?”
  2. “Tell us your city, the areas involved, and your preferred timing.”
  3. “Request your free estimate” with a link to the website.

How I will use them

I will reuse these structures with different examples. One Story sequence will be a unit of conversation, not a requirement to produce three brand-new images every day.

When an interesting response comes in, I will prepare a follow-up and explain what happened.

14Publishing criteria

Before publishing, I will review:

  • Whether the hook matches what the content delivers.
  • Whether the example is real or identified as a demonstration.
  • Whether the images make the explanation observable.
  • Whether claims match the product and the case.
  • Whether the service mentioned has been confirmed.
  • Whether there is a clear next action.

Simulations will be labeled. I will not identify the causes of stains, cracks, or peeling based only on appearance, nor will I present other people’s results as Picasso’s work.

15How I will measure results

Level What I will review Why
Attention Reach, views, and available playback metrics Understand what attracts interest
Usefulness Saves, shares, and relevant questions Identify helpful topics
Intent Profile visits, clicks, and inquiries Evaluate movement toward contact
Opportunity Inquiries within the service area and service scope Measure commercial fit
Sales Site visits, estimates, and booked jobs Connect content to the business
Operations Response times and production Adjust the workload

I will record results at seven and thirty days after each main post. I will separate organic from paid performance and compare posts at equivalent ages.

To attribute inquiries, I will use the information available: the post mentioned, the link used, or the prospect’s answer about how they heard of Picasso. Attribution will be approximate when data is missing.

16How I will decide what to repeat

During the first cycle, I will look to answer:

  • Which topics generate questions from homeowners?
  • Which hooks keep people watching?
  • Which content leads to relevant inquiries?
  • Which questions appear repeatedly?
  • Which formats can we produce consistently?

I will not consider a piece a winner solely because of its view count. Its evaluation will depend on the role assigned to it.

At the end of five weeks, I will propose which series to repeat, which openings to adjust, and which new questions to develop.

17What still needs to be confirmed with Picasso

To turn this preliminary plan into a final calendar, I will agree on:

  1. Service area and priority services.
  2. Available projects and examples.
  3. Technical lead and spokesperson.
  4. Filming, editing, and inquiry-management owners.
  5. Production dates and launch date.
  6. Access to performance analytics and sales tracking.

With these decisions in place, the proposal can move into production while keeping what we show, what we explain, and the services we offer aligned.
